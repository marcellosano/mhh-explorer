Compound Europe: interactive explorer for the compound-hazard results

A specification for a private, shareable web page that lets co-authors, reviewers and stakeholders explore the manuscript's compound-hazard results: single-hazard extremes, single-variable clusters and compound-event footprints, across five models, three scenarios and three time windows, with colour scales under the reader's control and every number traceable to a file.

Draft v0.1 · 18 September 2026 · prepared for Marcello Sanò and co-authors (Ferrario, Torresan, Critto) · companion to the clickable mockup
Status. Nothing is built beyond the mockup. This document is the proposal to approve, amend or reject. The mockup runs on real data for the compound layers and for one model's single-hazard layer; the cluster layer and the analogue cards are concept panels. Decisions needed from the lead author are collected at the end.

1. Purpose and audiences

One page, three uses. A co-author or referee checks a number, reproduces a figure, or tests how a definition or filter changes the picture. A scientist outside the team reads definitions, model agreement and parameters where they bite. A planner or civil-protection officer reads one map, one sentence and one place card in days per year, and leaves with a caption that carries its own caveats.

Brief modefive controls, the paper's colour scale locked, plain-language captions, storylines, place card. Default for first visits and phones.Full modeeverything: all models and statistics, comparison modes, filters, colour editor, cluster layer, sensitivity presets, provenance and the reconciliation ledger.

What no page can do. Re-run DBSCAN or thresholds (needs the 34 GB of masks), draw exact compound footprints (only centroid and extent are stored), show daily series, or fetch anything from outside its own files. 5. Rules that keep it honest

  • Every number names its statistic. The map's ensemble statistic and the published change factor are labelled separately; paired per-model ratios are always aggregated after pairing, never as a ratio of ensemble means.
  • No caption without an agreement clause. 5 of 5 “all five models agree”; 4 of 5 “four agree, one disagrees”; 3 of 5 “the models are split”; 2 or fewer: the sentence refuses to state a direction.
  • Three cell states: no events in either window (no colour), emergent (none in 1981–2010, some later), and change with a sign. Ratios are never shown for a zero baseline.
  • Days per year default to the union of event intervals per cell. The summed version (with multiplicity where events overlap) stays available and is labelled; on the current grids the two differ by 2–5 % in the Balkans and no cell exceeds 365.
  • Single-model, sensitivity and pooled views carry a badge on every number and in every copied caption.
  • The caveat line on every view: this counts days inside a hazard footprint; it says nothing about damage, exposure or people.
  • Reconciliation: the page recomputes the continental and regional factors from the shipped packs and shows them beside the published tables with PASS/FAIL at the printed precision of each cell.

7. Build phases

Phases are dependencies, not dates.

  1. Sandbox checks on the published mockup: sibling-file fetch (passed in the mockup), URL hash arriving through the viewer, clipboard, Web Worker from a blob (fallback: main-thread slices). Data contract written.
  2. Export spine and gates: grid, geometry, occupancy, events, published tables, provenance, manifest, self-check. Gates: land-cell count matches the paper's mask; occupancy counts reproduce the spatial-shift cells file; event counts reproduce the per-model disaggregation table; factors PASS.
  3. Map and colour engine (largely exists in the mockup): projections, legend histogram, editor, lock, badges.
  4. Workbench, compare, URL state, captions.
  5. Event explorer and worker re-rasterisation with the unfiltered-rebuild check printed on screen.
  6. Place card, headline, storylines, Brief mode.
  7. Single hazards: the one-time pass over the Vault masks for all five models (about one minute per model on this Mac) and the threshold inspector.
  8. Cluster layer exports and filters.
  9. Seasonality, shift, analogues from the published tables plus live recounts.
  10. Sensitivity and trust panel, reconciliation ledger.
  11. Phone, accessibility, performance pass; co-author read-through of all copy as text; adversarial attempt to build a misleading view; standalone build for a university page (folder build, or a single-file Atlas with the headline views only).

8. Not in v1, parked

  • Exact per-event cell footprints (needs a new pipeline pass writing cluster-to-cell membership; highest-value next addition).
  • Sensitivity presets for the other four models and other windows (droplet rerun).
  • Daily series, animations, case-study day frames.
  • Population, exposure, damage overlays; return periods; anything called “risk”.
  • ERA5 observed analogue overlays; Mann–Kendall trend maps as layers.
  • Town-name search (a 20 KB gazetteer is cheap; parked until the copy rules are settled).

9. Corrections found while specifying

  • The sensitivity sweep is thinner than any first draft assumed (MPI only, two windows, two degenerate settings). The page must read scope from the data, not assume it.
  • Days per year summed over overlapping events can exceed the union of intervals; the difference is small on the current grids but the union should be the default.
  • Rainfall thresholds and exceedances are stored in kg m⁻² s⁻¹; any rounding to fixed decimals destroys them. The export must keep significant digits.
  • Phase-2 grids run north-to-south, occupancy grids south-to-north; the contract fixes one cell order.
  • Wind extremes at 0.5° occur almost only over sea, so the single-hazard layer keeps sea cells and the windstorm story leans on pressure.
  • Regional factors in the paper use four overlapping bounding boxes, not AR6 polygons; the page must reproduce the boxes to reconcile.

10. Zoom and the data explorer (added 18 Sep, afternoon)

Zoom. Every map pans and zooms (scroll, drag, double-click to reset) in the mockup. The data are 0.5° cells, so zoom enlarges cells rather than adding detail; the hint under each map says so. Pinch on touch screens and a mini-map are build items.

Data explorer, five levels. A dedicated view opens any table the page holds, with a filter expression (dur>=10 & lat>50 & model==mpi), sortable columns, paging, row detail and copy-as-CSV (downloads once hosted outside Claude). Each map cell links into it.

3 · ClustersThe DBSCAN clusters each event was matched from, with the event → primary-cluster linkbuild≈12 MB packed rows (seven variables) + join key
4 · Yearly countsExtreme days per cell per year, 1981–2100, per model and variablebuild≈5 MB per variable, one more pass over the masks
5 · Daily valuesThe daily masks, exceedances and raw ISIMIP series for one cellneeds a data service34 GB stay on a server (droplet or university host); a small API answers one cell at a time; only possible once the page leaves Claude

Level 5 is the only one that changes the architecture: a static page cannot hold the daily data, so “down to the raw data” means static page plus a small read-only service next to the Vault copy. Everything else stays static.

11. Decisions for the lead author

  1. Scope of v1: all ten views as above, or the seven live ones first (0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, 9) with clusters and analogues following.
  2. Single-hazard pass over the Vault masks for all five models: in scope for v1 (recommended; it is the only exact-geometry layer).
  3. Default “days per year” definition: union of intervals (recommended) or summed event-days as in the current slides.
  4. Show the sensitivity sweep at all before the reviewer response is settled, and if so as the S7 tables present it (count ratio beside footprint and cell-day ratios, degenerate settings labelled).
  5. Cluster rows to ship: seven compound-relevant variables complete plus the largest rainfall clusters (≈12 MB), or summaries only.
  6. Pooled-model event views: allowed for distribution shape only and badged (recommended), or removed.
  7. Embargo and audience: private link to co-authors until acceptance; the university build waits for acceptance and a data DOI.
  8. Name, author line and citation block as they should appear on the page.
  9. Level 5 of the data explorer: a small read-only data service beside the Vault copy (droplet or university host) so a cell's daily series can be requested, or stop at level 4 and keep the platform fully static.
